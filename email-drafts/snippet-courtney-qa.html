Meet Our Field Secretary
Five Questions with Courtney Kirchoff

Foxhunter since 2006, endurance rider, designer and author, and the first person you’ll meet at every fixture this season.

Courtney and Nisha, her Arabian endurance mare, on the beach.

How did you first come to foxhunting, and what kept you coming back?

Back in 2006, I was struggling in the arena with my then seven-year-old Quarter Horse, who took laziness to the next level. A fellow boarder told me I should go foxhunting with them, as it would “liven him up.” I thought she was insane; nothing would liven him up.

Well, she was right. He transformed into a whole new horse with a ton of go, more go than I was ready for. The first half of that first season was terrifying, but after realizing I both survived and enjoyed each weekend, I was able to calm myself down and became addicted to the thrill of it. I now consider hunting part of my personality.

Tell us about your horses. Who are they, and what are they like in the hunt field?

I’m hunting two. My Arabian mare, Nisha, is an endurance champion who can eat 30-mile runs for breakfast. She’s a bit “fresh,” as we’d say, out in the field, because she doesn’t understand she has obligations beyond running for miles at a time. She’s a trail master but not the greatest jumper; she kind of wiggles over them, which isn’t super comfortable for me.

My second horse is Loch, an off-the-track Thoroughbred with a ton of hunting experience (not with me). He’s quite rateable and smart, and we’re currently working through some issues. He’s a jumping powerhouse, though, and I think he’d sail over anything put in front of him. My goal is for Loch to be my primary hunt horse.

What does a Field Secretary actually do?

I’ll be the main and first point of contact for guests and members when they arrive for check-in. Unless the universe conspires against me, I’ll be at the fixture early with a sign perched in my truck window: CHECK IN HERE.

I’ll ask you to sign a digital waiver and pay the appropriate capping fee before we ride off. Conveniently, it’s all digital now, so you can do everything from your phone. If you’re brand new, I’m happy to introduce you to the field masters and help you decide which flight is best for you and your horse.

What’s one piece of advice you’d give someone capping with us for the first time?

Every horse loves foxhunting; it taps into their herd instincts. So don’t be surprised if you have a “new” horse out in the field the first time. Think of them like an excited child at Disneyland who can’t wait to hit the fun rides: the more you try to overcontrol them, the harder it will be. Relax and have fun. Calm legs, calm mind. And partake in the stirrup cup when we have it.

Away from the hunt field you’re a graphic designer, and some of the hunt’s new graphics are yours. What do you love about bringing the two together?

I’m a Jacqueline of many trades right now; graphic design is one of them, and I’m also an author. Foxhunting is probably the sport I love most, so combining a skill set with a passion is a win-win. I’d like to introduce more people to hunting, and design is a form of communication. This is what we’d call “alignment.”
