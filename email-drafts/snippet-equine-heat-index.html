Horse Care
Riding in the Heat

Horses shed heat mostly by sweating, and humidity makes sweat less effective. That’s why the number to watch is the heat index, the “feels like” temperature in your weather app, rather than the thermometer alone.

Check it before you load up, and plan your ride around it.

Under 80°
Little to no risk
Ride as usual. Your normal cool-down is enough: water, a quick hose and somewhere airy to dry.
81–90°
Slight risk
Still fine to ride, but take the cool-down seriously. Walk until breathing settles, then water and hose before putting away.
91–100°
Elevated risk
Shorten and soften the work. Plan on extra cooling afterward, with shade or fans, until temperature, pulse and breathing are back to normal.
101–110°
Very high risk
Don’t ride. A horse can’t cool itself effectively at this point. If work can’t be avoided, hose continuously afterward in moving air until vital signs recover.
Over 110°
Extreme risk
Horses can overheat standing still. No exercise. Keep them sheltered with fans or misters, plenty of fresh water and active cooling all day.

Know the warning signs: heavy breathing that doesn’t settle, a horse that stops sweating, dullness or stumbling. If you see them, stop, cool hard with water and call your vet.

Bands adapted from a widely shared equine heat index chart. When in doubt, ask your vet.
